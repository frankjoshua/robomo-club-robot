How to read it

  • Grey bars = the 5 holes in one column+band, already connected on the board. Never land two nets on the same bar — that's a short. Every part here sits in its own columns for that reason.
  • The board is landscape: 30 columns, top band F–J and bottom band A–E split by the trench. Rails run the top and bottom edges; their + / − markings alternate. Here: top + = 5 V, top − = GND, bottom + = 3.3 V, bottom − = GND. Match by the printed +/−, and tie the two GND rails together once (col 30).
  • Mount the Teensy on female headers across columns 2–15, straddling the trench — bottom pins in the A–E band, top pins in the F–J band. Each pin owns its column's 5-hole bar; jumper from any free hole in that bar.
  • 3.3 V on the SPI/encoder/OLED side; only the 5 V rail and pin 8 → Sabertooth are 5 V.
  • On/Off is a pad on the Teensy underside — flying-lead it to the reset switch (col 17, row E). The switch straddles the trench; its other leg (row F) → GND. A Jetson GPIO drives the same pad through an NPN for remote reset.
  • Dark holes = occupied Teensy pins; colored holes = jumper endpoints. Slide the cluster along the columns to taste — only relative placement matters.
