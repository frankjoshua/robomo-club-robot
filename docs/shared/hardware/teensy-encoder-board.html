Notes

  • 3.3 V everywhere on the SPI side. The Teensy 4.0 is not 5 V tolerant. Power the encoder board from the Teensy 3V3 pin so SCK/MOSI/MISO/CS are all 3.3 V. Only VIN (5 V bus) and the pin 8 → Sabertooth serial live in the 5 V world.
  • Verify the encoder supply voltage. The board runs at 3.3 V here. If your wheel encoders are 5 V parts they may misread on 3.3 V or over-drive the LS7366R inputs — check the encoder datasheet before closing the box.
  • Dead-encoder suspects. Both chips read a flat 0x00, which points at VCC / GND / MISO to the encoder board rather than the SPI bus. Give those joints extra care and re-run the firmware self-test (it reports each chip's config register on /diagnostics).
  • Reset = the On/Off pad, not RESET. Pulling On/Off to GND toggles board power. Because the encoder board is fed from the Teensy's 3.3 V, an On/Off power-cycle also resets the encoder chips — one button fixes both the stuck micro-ROS session and a hung LS7366R.
  • 4-pin tactile gotcha. The two pins on the same side are permanently tied. Wire one side to On/Off and the opposite side to GND, or the switch does nothing / is always closed.
  • Remote reset (Tier 2). Drive On/Off from a Jetson GPIO through an NPN (GPIO → 1 kΩ → base, collector → On/Off, emitter → GND) so the Jetson only switches the pad, never drives voltage into it. Then the reset script becomes "pulse a GPIO." The button stays wired in parallel.
  • Mount on headers. Socket the Teensy and the encoder board on female headers, bring every off-board wire to screw terminals, so field service is tool-only and either board pulls out.
  • Optional OLED on 18/19 shows session status, live encoder counts/RPM, LS7366R self-test, and later AUTO/MANUAL mode — diagnose the robot with no laptop.
